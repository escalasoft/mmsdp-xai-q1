# Preliminary manuscript text — E1 CODE, ActiveMQ/T1

## Global CODE attribution results

We analysed the accepted, persisted E1 CODE explanations for 245 ActiveMQ T1 commits and six features. All 20 deterministic attribution repetitions were identical; therefore, distributional summaries use one vector per commit (n = 245), rather than treating repeated computations as independent observations. Logistic regression (LR) used a centred analytical logit decomposition. Random forest (RF) used exact path-dependent TreeSHAP on the positive-class probability scale; XGBoost (XGB) and LightGBM (LGB) used exact path-dependent TreeSHAP on their native margin scales. Attribution magnitudes were summarised within each model only.

LR ranked ent first (mean absolute contribution 0.490638 logit units), followed by nd (0.186846) and nf (0.181620). In contrast, all three tree ensembles ranked la and ld first and second. Their respective mean absolute contributions were 0.088517 and 0.033489 for RF, 0.755547 and 0.146422 for XGB, and 0.869414 and 0.199690 for LGB. The leading two features accounted for 66.16% of LR's total mean absolute attribution, and 61.96%, 76.35%, and 72.69% for RF, XGB, and LGB. These dimensionless concentration measures describe the distribution within each model, not equivalent amounts of predictive effect across scales.

The strongest global rank concordance was observed between RF and LGB (Spearman rho = 0.829; top-three Jaccard overlap = 1.0). LR had negative rank correlations with RF, XGB, and LGB (−0.543, −0.486, and −0.829, respectively). RF–XGB and XGB–LGB correlations were 0.600 and 0.429. These are descriptive comparisons over six aligned features, without significance testing. Differences cannot be attributed solely to model architecture: the models, explanation functions and reference conventions also differ.

Global rankings concealed commit-level variation. ent was the largest absolute contribution for 192 of 245 LR explanations, whereas la dominated 183 RF, 193 XGB, and 198 LGB explanations. Median cancellation indices, defined as 1 − |sum(phi)|/sum(|phi|), were 0.427, 0.154, 0.167, and 0.225. Thus, positive and negative contributions can offset each other, and a small net shift need not imply uniformly small feature contributions. No row had an all-zero attribution vector. Distributional tables and plots retain all observations and report both exact zeros and a numerical zero band of ±1e−12; they do not infer unobserved feature values.

## Local explanation results

The eight local commits were fixed before this synthesis and were neither replaced nor selected by model outputs. All 32 model–case combinations had stored positive-class probabilities below 0.5. This subset consequently illustrates negative threshold decisions; it does not characterise positive alerts or establish predictive correctness, because labels are not used here.

For case 3 (f2653e69362cef9de49e6b0a5a248c7ad0602b6b), ent supplied LR's largest positive contribution, whereas la supplied the largest positive contribution in each tree model. For case 5 (919099b419c2d2e355883c6d01df6a9854735d6d), all six tree-model contributions were non-positive, while LR had a positive nf contribution. For case 6 (741b739da88a72cc9bb0f8314b6a2e39a60b78da), stored probabilities ranged from 0.00412 for LGB to 0.20171 for LR, although all models remained below the threshold; la was the largest negative contribution in each tree model. Case 2 additionally shows why contribution sign is relative to the explanation base: LGB had no negative feature contributions, yet its probability was only 0.26479. Positive contributions do not necessarily imply a positive threshold decision.

The complete local tables provide each commit identity, model identity, base value, six signed contributions, native output, reconstruction, residual, probability, and largest positive/negative contributions. Original feature values were not present in the exclusive authorised result source and are explicitly unavailable; no values were reverse-engineered from the explanations. Waterfalls therefore show contributions only.

## Fidelity and deterministic stability

Independent reconstruction from the persisted arrays verified all 245 commits in all 20 repetitions for every model. Maximum absolute additive residuals were 4.44e−16 (LR), 1.75e−14 (RF), 3.22e−6 (XGB), and 1.07e−14 (LGB). The XGB residual satisfies the frozen scaled tolerance: its maximum |residual|/(1+|output|) was 8.23e−7, below 1e−6. LR met its stricter 1e−10 scaled residual criterion. Attribution differences across repetitions were zero, and the persisted stability diagnostics covered all 190 repetition pairs per model. These observations establish numerical fidelity and repeatability for the fixed models, population and reference. They do not establish causal validity or robustness to alternative populations, references or perturbations.

## E1 CODE within MMSDP-XAI

E1 CODE provides feature-level explanations of individual unimodal CODE predictors. Conceptually, E1 TEXT occupies the same explanatory level for a different input modality, whereas E2 addresses modality contributions at the fusion level. This synthesis makes no new empirical comparison with TEXT or E2, whose results were outside its exclusive input source. Unimodal feature attributions must not be added across modalities or substituted for E2 fusion contributions.

The persisted explanation–output identities enable an auditor to trace a prediction to its model-specific additive decomposition and examine counteracting contributions or model disagreement. Stored threshold decisions differed in 12–27 of the 245 commits across model pairs; without labels, this quantifies disagreement rather than comparative accuracy. These diagnostics could inform a future QA investigation of model-dependent decisions, but this study did not evaluate reviewer effectiveness, defect discovery gains, workload reduction, industrial benefits or increased trust. Such claims would require separate empirical validation.

## Limitations

The analysis is restricted to ActiveMQ/T1 and cannot be generalised automatically to the remaining projects. LR logit, RF probability and boosted-tree native margins are different scales. Even dimensionless profile and rank comparisons remain descriptive because references and explanation semantics differ. Additive fidelity is not causality, and deterministic stability is not perturbation robustness. The 245 commits are one fixed temporal population; no sampling-based confidence intervals or hypothesis tests are asserted. The local cases are preselected and all fall below the decision threshold, limiting their coverage of prediction regimes.

Original feature values are absent from the authorised package; accordingly, feature-value-coloured beeswarms, dependence plots and high-versus-low feature interpretations are omitted. Potential correlation between original features cannot be measured from attribution vectors alone. PROCESS remains outside the current E1 extension because independent temporal provenance is unproven, not because leakage was demonstrated or because PROCESS was excluded from the predictive framework. The analysis retains the provenance limitations of reconstructed pipelines and the accepted source results. No models, SHAP computations, calibration or T2 data were accessed during synthesis.
